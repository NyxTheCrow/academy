# 01 · Feedback & Numberlessness

The single most important system. Academagia's failure was that its feedback
loop *was* the numbers. Here, the numbers hide and the **feedback carries the
charm**.

## The core rule [DECIDED]

- The simulation runs on real numbers underneath.
- **Player mode shows none of them.** State is read qualitatively.
- **Dev mode shows the numbers** (and doubles as a transparency/assist toggle).

## Information as reward — skill-gated *resolution*

A hidden skill doesn't (only) decide pass/fail; it decides **how much and how
precisely the world reveals itself**. As a skill rises, descriptions sharpen.
Examples raised:

- **Sensing your own mana:** unskilled → fuzzy *"low? high?"*; better → clearer
  bands; eventually → precise readings (even a percentage). The *resolution* of
  self-knowledge is the progression.
- **Mana theory / recognition:** low → *"something arcane is happening"*; high →
  *recognize the exact spell at a glance*, and its components.
- **Insight / body-language:** higher → **read people**; you're simply told more
  of what they feel or hide.
- **Perception:** higher → more environmental detail surfaced in prose.

> Design pattern: many outcomes should be a *graded reveal* — the same
> underlying fact rendered at N levels of clarity depending on the relevant
> skill. Write content as "here is the fact; here are 3–4 fuzzier framings of
> it," and pick the framing by skill.

## Diegetic feedback channels (instead of stat numbers)

- **Grades & teacher comments** instead of a "skill: 42" — you learn you're good
  at wards because the instructor says so, or from your marks.
- **Bodily sensation** for resources (see `02`) — "drained," "buzzing," "raw."
- **Social read-outs** — what an NPC's posture/tone tells you, gated by insight.
- **Reactions & consequences** — the world responding is the score.

## The optimizer problem (and answers)

Raised (qhaqx): after a few playthroughs people **optimize**, and opaque
feedback becomes frustrating; unclear feedback can feel bad. Mitigations, in
order of preference:

1. **Divination as in-fiction optimization** [LATER, promising]. A divination
   skill that previews **likely outcomes / possible "fates"** — reframing
   Brante's fate-aiming as a *magic skill you can invest in*. Higher divination
   → clearer forecasts. This turns the urge to optimize into gameplay instead of
   fighting it.
2. **Dev / transparency mode** [BUILT] — numbers on demand for those who want
   them.
3. **Brante-style qualitative bands** — words with short descriptions and, where
   useful, a sense of the range you're aiming at.

## Tension to hold consciously [OPEN]

Charm wants **opacity**; mastery wants **feedback**. The lean: keep the *surface*
textual, but always offer an **in-fiction way to earn sharper information**
(study, divination, careful observation, dev mode). Mastery should feel
*earned*, never *hidden*.
