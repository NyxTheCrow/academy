# 04 · Setting & Premise

## The central conundrum

Two flavors of magic academy. Different **core conflicts**; long-term both are
worth doing, but the first one built shapes everything.

### A) Cozy / political traditional academy (life-sim base)
- The school is **safe**; classes are **structured**; strong **social / VN**
  layer.
- Conflict comes from **classism, petty rivalries, noble politics** — not the
  environment trying to kill you.
- **Danger is opt-in**, living in **dungeoneering / dueling / missions**.

### B) Deadly cursed "murder-school"
- **Necromancer-cursed halls**; corridors and rooms can kill you; lethal things
  lying around.
- Edgy / horror; **dark magic** and **sanity** front and center.

## The call [DECIDED — provisional]

**Build A first.** Reasons:
- It's the natural base for a **minute-to-minute life sim**, and a life sim
  *needs* a social layer.
- Friends leaned **cozy + political over edgy**.
- **B is compatible later** — as a separate mode, an alternate campaign, or even
  an **endgame turn** ("the school *becomes* the murder-school"). The two aren't
  exclusive; they're just different first foundations, and A is the base that
  carries the incremental daily loop.

## Thematic seeds for A (the interesting friction)

- **Class & blood.** A magocratic nobility hold "proper" magic; commoners are
  constrained. Navigating that hierarchy is core texture.
- **State wards.** Poor but talented students are taken in as **state wards**,
  bound to future **state service** — effectively indentured "employee-slaves."
  Personal stake: your **autonomy**.
- **Fake descent** (*limpieza de sangre*). A path/background where you **pass as
  noble** on fabricated lineage; **discovery risks ruin, imprisonment, death.**
  A long-burning tension engine. (See `05` — works as background *and* as an
  arc that unfolds in the campaign.)
- **Student union / rebellion.** Students are restless; organized student
  politics you can be pulled into.
- **"Dealing with the nobles' bullshit"** as a commoner is proposed as *the*
  friction that makes daily life meaningful.

## Danger channels (kept out of everyday life in A)

- **Dungeoneering** — and crucially, **failing can mean being rescued by others**
  → a built-in **social hook** (debts, bonds, obligations).
- **Dueling.**
- **Missions.**

## Note

Premise is rich and flexible; the real work is **deciding the core stakes** so
the daily loop has weight. Current lean: *autonomy vs. a class system that owns
you*, played out through school life.
